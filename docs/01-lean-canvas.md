# Lean Canvas

**Canvas status:** Version 0.2 — segment selected; assumptions require validation
**Primary hypothesis:** Managed lead follow-up and client onboarding automation
**Review trigger:** After every five customer interviews

| Area | Current hypothesis | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|
| Customer segments | Owner-led consultancies or specialist agencies with 2–25 staff | Segment-specific interviews and workflow observations |
| Early adopters | Firms already using a CRM or shared inbox but still coordinating follow-up manually | Examples of active workarounds and budget ownership |
| Top problems | Slow/inconsistent lead response; incomplete qualification; fragmented onboarding; poor status visibility | Frequency, time cost, lost revenue, and emotional urgency |
| Existing alternatives | Founder/admin effort, CRM reminders, generic automation tools, agencies, doing nothing | Current spend, satisfaction, switching constraints |
| Unique value proposition | Reliable lead-to-onboarding operations without hiring an automation team | Customer language and comparative buying criteria |
| High-level concept | A managed operations layer combining workflow software, AI assistance, and human control | Reaction during interviews and pilot conversion |
| Solution | Standard intake, enrichment, qualification, drafted follow-up, approval, scheduling, onboarding checklist, exception queue | Manual pilot completion and measurable result |
| Channels | Founder-led outreach, trusted advisors, software/accounting partners, narrow educational content | Response, meeting, proposal, and close rates by channel |
| Revenue streams | Setup fee plus recurring managed-service subscription | Paid pilots at two or more price points |
| Cost structure | Founder delivery time, AI/API usage, software, hosting, support, professional services | Actual pilot unit economics |
| Key metrics | Qualified leads handled, response time, conversion, onboarding cycle time, exception rate, founder minutes, gross margin, churn | Baseline and post-service measurement |
| Unfair advantage | Accumulated workflow knowledge, evaluation data, reliable integrations, and trust within one vertical | Retention and performance improvement over time |

## Riskiest assumptions

1. The problem is painful enough to fund from an existing budget.
2. A narrow workflow can be standardized across customers in one segment.
3. Customers will trust a supervised service with required system access.
4. Faster follow-up or onboarding produces a measurable business outcome.
5. The service can reach a healthy margin without hiding founder labor.

## Validation experiments

| Experiment | Pass criterion | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Candidate register | 20–30 suitable firms/people with source and outreach path | Deferred — required |
| First interview batch | Five completed structured interviews | Deferred — required |
| 15–20 problem interviews | At least 8 describe the same costly workflow without prompting | Deferred — required |
| Workflow observation | At least 5 customers demonstrate actual steps and artifacts | Not started |
| Offer test | At least 3 qualified prospects request a proposal | Not started |
| Paid pilot | At least 1 customer pays before custom software is built | Not started |
| Repeatability test | 80% of delivery steps shared across 3 pilots | Not started |
| Economics test | Positive contribution margin with founder time fully recorded | Not started |

## Pivot conditions

Revisit the thesis when interview evidence shows low urgency, low willingness to
pay, excessive customer-specific variation, unacceptable data risk, or no credible
path to positive unit economics.
